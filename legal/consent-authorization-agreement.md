## 1. What you're authorizing

By signing this Agreement (or clicking "I agree" at signup), you authorize the Operator,
and the specific staff the Operator designates to work on your account, to:

1. **Search for and identify job openings** on your behalf, using the target job titles,
   locations, salary floor, and other preferences you provide during intake.
2. **Prepare tailored application materials** on your behalf — a version of your resume/CV
   selected and reworded from the truthful information you provide, and draft answers to
   common screening questions — for each job opening that meets the qualification
   criteria discussed with you.
3. **Submit job applications on your behalf** to the specific job openings the Operator
   identifies as a match, using the materials described above, up to the volume included
   in your subscription tier.
4. **Communicate with employers or their applicant-tracking systems in your name** where
   reasonably necessary to complete a submission (e.g., creating a submission record,
   answering a platform's required screening questions) — see Section 4 for the specific
   scope limit on this.

This authorization does **not** extend to accepting a job offer, negotiating compensation,
signing an employment contract, or making any binding commitment on your behalf. Those
decisions remain yours alone, and the Operator will not take them for you.

## 2. Why applications are submitted manually, by a human, one at a time

This section exists because it's a deliberate design choice, not a limitation, and it's
worth understanding as part of what you're authorizing:

- Most job boards and applicant-tracking systems have Terms of Service that prohibit
  automated or bulk application submission ("bots"). Accounts and applicant profiles
  that are flagged as automated risk being **rate-limited, suppressed, or banned** —
  and that risk attaches to *your* profile and application history, not just the tool
  used to submit it.
- To stay on the safe side of that line, a human member of the Operator's team reviews
  and manually submits each application, at normal human pace, through the direct
  apply-link the matching/tailoring process generates. Software may assist in finding
  matches and drafting materials, but the final click-to-submit step on each individual
  application is done by a person.
- **What this means for you:** your applications go out looking like — and being —
  ordinary individual submissions, not mass/bulk activity, which protects your standing
  with the platforms and employers you're applying to. It also means volume is bounded by
  human working hours, which is why your subscription tier specifies a monthly
  application count rather than "unlimited."

## 4. Scope limits and account creation

- The Operator will only submit applications to job openings that were evaluated against
  the qualification criteria established for you and communicated to you as part of the
  service — not speculative or mismatched applications.
- **Account creation on job platforms is in scope.** Where a job posting requires an
  account on the employer's or platform's own application system before you can apply, you
  authorize the Operator to create that account on your behalf, using your name, email
  address, and other information necessary to complete the application, as part of the
  application process described in Section 1.
- The Operator will not use your information to apply to positions outside the scope of
  what you've directed (target titles/locations) or to positions on your stated exclusion
  list (companies, industries, or deal-breakers you've flagged).

## 4a. Credentials for platform accounts created on your behalf

Because the Operator will be creating and using logins on third-party job platforms in
your name, the credentials for those accounts are sensitive — this section states plainly
how they're handled, matched to what's actually implemented (not a stricter or looser
promise than the real system):

- Passwords for accounts the Operator creates on your behalf are generated and stored in a
  dedicated password manager, not written into your resume/intake data file. The
  structured record the Operator keeps about you (skills, screening answers, application
  tracker, etc.) holds, at most, a **reference pointer** to where a given account's
  credentials live in the password manager — never the raw password itself.
- Access to the password manager is restricted to the staff actually working on your
  account.
- Platform accounts created on your behalf are created and managed under an
  **Operator-controlled email alias**, not your personal email address. The Operator
  administers these accounts independently as part of performing the service — they are,
  practically speaking, Operator-controlled on your behalf, not accounts you can log into
  or recover directly yourself using your own email. This makes the commitment below —
  that you receive the credentials back on cancellation — the operative mechanism by which
  you actually gain independent access, rather than a formality.
- **On cancellation:** you are entitled to reclaim access to any platform account created
  in your name — the Operator will provide you the credentials (or reset the password and
  give you the new one) for each such account when your subscription ends, rather than
  retaining sole access to accounts created under your identity.

### Flag for the operator — this is a real security/liability question, not just a legal footnote

Creating and holding login credentials to third-party platforms, under a real client's
name and personal information, is a materially different risk profile than storing resume
text:

- **Breach exposure:** if the password manager or the Operator's own systems were
  compromised, what's exposed isn't just contact info — it's active logins to accounts
  that themselves hold the client's name, email, application history, and possibly resume
  copies uploaded directly to that platform. Most US states have data-breach notification
  laws that can be triggered by exactly this kind of exposure (credentials + PII in
  combination). This needs real security review (access controls, MFA on the password
  manager itself, an actual incident-response plan) and likely attorney input on what
  breach-notification obligations would apply — not something this document can discharge
  on its own.
- **Account ownership, now resolved as a real operational fact, not just phrasing:**
  because these accounts run under an Operator-controlled alias rather than the client's
  own email, the client has no independent way to access or recover an account created in
  their name during an active subscription — the cancellation handback (confirmed,
  published as-is, no exceptions) is the client's only path to independent control. That
  makes the handback commitment load-bearing rather than a nice-to-have: if it's ever
  skipped or delayed in practice, a client is left with accounts that exist under their
  identity on job platforms that neither they nor, after cancellation, the Operator is
  actively managing. Recommend this commitment be operationally checklisted at offboarding
  (not just stated in this document), so it's honored in practice every time, not just on
  paper.
- **Don't overstate the security posture in client-facing copy.** The honest description
  here is "generated and stored in a password manager, access restricted to staff on your
  account" — resist the temptation to dress this up as "bank-level encryption" or similar
  in any marketing/consent copy; overstating security measures is its own
  truth-in-advertising exposure if it doesn't match what's actually running.

## 5. No guarantee of interviews, offers, or employment

**outcome**. The Operator will apply the qualification process consistently and submit
applications on your behalf as described above; the Operator does not and cannot guarantee
that any application will result in a response, interview, or job offer. See the Refund
Policy for what *is* backed by a specific, bounded commitment.
